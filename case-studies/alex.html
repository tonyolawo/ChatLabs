Alex: chat-based finance for Kenyan small businesses

How a chat-first finance assistant for Kenyan shop owners was evaluated and designed across web, mobile, WhatsApp, Telegram, and SMS.

The problem

Small hardware and retail businesses in Kenya run their day-to-day finances (invoices, sales reports, stock, customers, tax) while living on WhatsApp, Telegram, and SMS. Existing accounting tools are desktop-first and heavy, and a busy owner won't sit in front of a dashboard. The finance workflow needs to meet the owner where they already are: inside the chats they use all day.

Problem evaluation

The work began with an interviewing-style review of a real owner's workflow, using a sample shop (Rainbow Hardware) to ground every scenario. That review surfaced the flows that actually matter for a shop like this:

  • Creating invoices and receiving M-Pesa confirmations
  • Weekly and monthly sales reporting
  • Stock low alerts and restocking
  • Customer lookup and credit
  • VAT filing and KRA eTIMS compliance
  • Storefront orders

The key finding: the assistant must feel like a conversation, not a queue of forms. Owners won't fill out a structured dashboard screen. They will send a message and expect a useful reply.

The design

Alex is a single assistant engine (avatar "A") with each channel styled natively. The same intentions and data sit behind every interface:

  • A web chat with a side dashboard
  • A mobile-first app UI
  • WhatsApp with interactive buttons and read receipts
  • Telegram with bot commands and inline keyboards
  • SMS with numbered text menus

Two visual themes (fintech and casual) ship with a live toggle, and a shared sample dataset keeps every channel telling the same story about the same shop.

Where it stands now

Interactive prototypes exist for all five channels, plus a landing page that explains the product and collects waitlist signups. The demo and landing page are live for walkthroughs and feedback; implementation and real integrations are the next phase.
